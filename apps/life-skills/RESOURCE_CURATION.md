# Life Skills Resource Curation Process

The proposed approach is a reviewed resource catalogue linked to the app's 88 topics and 440 subtopics. Search rankings help discover material, but do not determine what the app teaches.

This document records the resource-selection proposal. It does not mean that research agents, scheduled maintenance or content approval workflows have been implemented.

## A1 — Define what each lesson must teach

For every subtopic, record:

- The practical skill the learner should demonstrate.
- Their starting knowledge, age group and reading level.
- Relevant country or jurisdiction.
- The evidence needed to support the lesson.

For example, Budgeting needs resources that teach income, essential spending, irregular costs and savings, followed by an exercise that checks whether the learner can build a workable budget.

These are starting points, not a complete approved source list. Each individual resource must be assessed for the intended lesson.

| Subject area | Starting source | Why investigate it |

| Communication and workplace conflict | [Acas training](https://www.acas.org.uk/training) | Workplace guidance and conflict-management training. |
| Digital safety and privacy | [National Cyber Security Centre](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/section/advice-guidance/you-your-family) | Guidance for individuals and families. |
| First aid | [St John Ambulance](https://www.sja.org.uk/get-advice/) | Structured first-aid guidance and accessible resources. |
| Broader learning content | [Open University OpenLearn](https://university.open.ac.uk/open-educational-resources/openlearn/free-courses) | Courses with defined learning outcomes and downloadable formats. |

For Emotional Intelligence, examine research behind specific claims. An engaging article is not sufficient evidence that an exercise improves a particular outcome.

For each learning outcome, search for relevant official guidance, original research or evidence syntheses, and practical teaching resources. Separate the evidence supporting a claim from the material used to explain or practise it.

## A3 — Assess resources consistently

Use this proposed scoring system:

| Code | Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|---:|
| C1 | Author expertise and supporting evidence | 30% |
| C2 | Fit to the lesson's learning outcome | 25% |
| C3 | Practical exercises and assessment value | 20% |
| C4 | Currency and jurisdictional relevance | 15% |
| C5 | Accessibility and clarity | 10% |

Record the reasons for each score. A high aggregate score must not override a critical factual, safety or jurisdictional problem.

Reuse permission is a separate publication gate. A high score does not override an unsuitable licence. Free access does not automatically permit inclusion in a commercial app.

For example, [OpenLearn's copyright policy](https://about.open.ac.uk/strategy-and-policies/policies-and-statements/copyright-openlearn) generally restricts reuse of its materials to non-commercial purposes. Additional restrictions can apply to individual assets. Check the specific resource and intended use before copying, adapting or embedding it. Use original explanations and permitted citations or links where appropriate.

## A4 — Keep evidence attached to every lesson

Record the following information in the catalogue:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Topic and subtopic IDs | Connect the resource to the existing curriculum. |
| Learning outcome | Identify the skill the resource supports. |
| Resource URL and title | Identify the exact source. |
| Author and publisher | Establish provenance and relevant expertise. |
| Supporting claims | Identify which statements or exercises depend on the source. |
| Publication, update and retrieval dates | Support currency checks and later review. |
| Jurisdiction and learner suitability | Prevent inappropriate application across countries or audiences. |
| Licence and permitted use | Record attribution, adaptation and commercial-use conditions. |
| Reviewer and approval status | Distinguish discovered, reviewed and publishable resources. |
| Next review date | Make future review accountable. |

Important claims should have independent corroboration where available. Several sites repeating the same underlying source are not independent corroboration. Conflicting sources should trigger review rather than being combined into an apparently certain answer.

## A5 — Assign the DSDM agents clear responsibilities

These are proposed responsibilities for the content workflow.

| Agent | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Research Agent | Discover candidate resources and record provenance. |
| Subject Reviewer | Check accuracy, evidence and suitability. |
| Rights Reviewer | Check attribution, adaptation and commercial-use permissions. |
| Learning Designer | Create original explanations, exercises and assessments from approved evidence. |
| QA Agent | Verify citations, calculations, accessibility and lesson coverage. |
| Maintenance Agent | Detect broken links and changed guidance, then flag affected lessons for review. |

AI can accelerate discovery and drafting. Medical, financial, legal and physical-safety material should require qualified human review before publication. Agent checks do not replace qualified review.

The proposed publication sequence is discovery, evidence review, rights review, lesson design, QA and approval. Changes to a supporting source should flag the affected lessons for reassessment. Review frequency should depend on the topic's risk and rate of change.

## A6 — Validate the process with one complete topic

Start with Emotional Intelligence:

1. Define the outcomes for its five subtopics.
2. Gather and assess candidate resources.
3. Resolve unsupported or conflicting claims in the supplied draft material.
4. Approve evidence and reuse permissions.
5. Produce original lessons, exercises and assessments.
6. Test learner understanding and record the review evidence.

Scale the process across all 440 subtopics after this complete topic demonstrates that the workflow produces accurate, usable and maintainable learning material.
