# DSDM — Philosophy & The Eight Principles

## Philosophy

> **"Best business value emerges when projects are aligned to clear business
> goals, deliver frequently and involve the collaboration of motivated and
> empowered people."**

Everything in DSDM flows from this statement. If a practice, decision, or
behaviour does not support it, DSDM treats it as a candidate for removal.

## The Eight Principles

The eight principles are non-negotiable. Compromising on any one of them
undermines the philosophy and introduces risk to the project. They are the
"do not break these" rules of the framework.

### 1. Focus on the business need

Every decision is taken with the question *"does this serve the business
need?"* The team understands the true business priorities, establishes a sound
**business case**, and guarantees the **Minimum Usable Subset** (the Must Haves)
is delivered. Build *what is needed, when it is needed* — no gold-plating.

### 2. Deliver on time

Delivering on time is often the single most important success factor.
Timeboxes are fixed; **time is never traded away**. Predictable delivery builds
trust and confidence. This principle is enabled by timeboxing, MoSCoW
prioritisation, and protecting the agreed deadlines.

### 3. Collaborate

Teams that collaborate out-perform groups of individuals working in silos.
DSDM actively involves the right stakeholders, at the right time, throughout
the project, and encourages a **one-team culture** across business and
technical people. Empowerment of the team is essential.

### 4. Never compromise quality

The level of quality to be delivered is **agreed at the start**. All work
should be aimed at achieving that level — no more and no less. Quality is fixed,
so the solution must be *good enough* and fit for business purpose. This is
supported by testing early and continuously, reviews, and clear acceptance
criteria.

### 5. Build incrementally from firm foundations

DSDM advocates **enough design up front (EDUF)** — just enough to create firm
foundations and a shared understanding of scope, *without* the analysis
paralysis of "big design up front." Once foundations exist, the solution is
built incrementally, allowing early realisation of business value and
re-prioritisation as understanding grows.

### 6. Develop iteratively

DSDM uses **iterative development** and continuous feedback to converge on an
accurate business solution. Nothing is built perfectly the first time; the team
embraces change as a way to refine the solution. *"Rome was not built in a day."*

### 7. Communicate continuously and clearly

Poor communication is frequently cited as the biggest cause of project failure.
DSDM promotes:
- Facilitated workshops
- Daily stand-ups
- Modelling and prototyping (visual, low-ambiguity communication)
- Lean, timely documentation
- Encouraging informal, face-to-face communication

### 8. Demonstrate control

The project must be **visibly under control** at all times. This means proactive
management, making plans and progress visible to all, measuring progress through
*delivery of products* (not just activity), and using objective, evidence-based
control. The **Project Manager** and **Technical Coordinator** rely on this to
keep governance and stakeholders assured.

## Why the principles matter

| Principle | Primary benefit |
|-----------|-----------------|
| 1. Focus on the business need | Right thing gets built |
| 2. Deliver on time | Predictability & trust |
| 3. Collaborate | Better, faster decisions |
| 4. Never compromise quality | Fit-for-purpose solution |
| 5. Build incrementally from firm foundations | Early value, manageable risk |
| 6. Develop iteratively | Accuracy through feedback |
| 7. Communicate continuously and clearly | Fewer misunderstandings |
| 8. Demonstrate control | Confident governance |

> **Rule of thumb:** If you find yourself about to break a principle to "save
> the project," stop. Breaking a principle is a warning sign that the project's
> assumptions (scope, resourcing, or engagement) need to be renegotiated — not
> that the principle is wrong.
